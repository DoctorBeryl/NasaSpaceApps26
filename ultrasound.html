How a guided scan works

  1. Before flight, a sonographer scans each astronaut and records the ideal probe position for each organ.
  2. In flight, a camera tracks the probe against markers on the body and compares it with that record.
  3. ASTRA turns the biggest difference into one instruction: slide, turn, or tilt.
  4. An image model checks the organ is in view, saves the frame, and sends it to Earth for a radiologist to read.

Real systems this follows

VULCAN by Tienovix guides a novice's probe with augmented-reality overlays and lets a remote expert review the scan.

EchoFinder-2 (ESA and CNES) was tested on the ISS: shapes on a tablet overlap and turn green when the probe is in place, then AI identifies the organ and saves the image.

What's simulated here

  • The probe follows your mouse or keys, not a tracking camera.
  • The scan image is generated, not a real ultrasound.
  • Guidance is geometry against a stored target, not a trained image model.
